SEO Backlinks: Article Platforms Where You Can Publish Yourself

SEO · Guide · By MyToolster

Directories are the slow lane for backlinks. Article platforms are the fast lane: you publish, you get the link, and good content gets indexed within days. Here are the platforms I've actually published on — with indexing speed ranking, how to boost indexing odds, and FAQ.

1. The lineup

  • Medium — high domain authority. Set your profile bio with your links, organize posts into public Lists, engage under already-indexed articles in your niche.
  • Dev.to — developer-focused, great for tutorials. Canonical URLs supported for cross-posting.
  • Hashnode — dev blogging with your own subdomain. Warning: AutoMod aggressively flags promotional "tool list" posts on free subdomains — write tutorials, keep same-domain links to 1–2 per post.
  • Substack — newsletter + blog in one. Posts get emailed to subscribers and stay publicly indexed.
  • Blogger Qiita WordPress — Google's own platform, indexes fast. Simple editor, zero friction.
  • Hacker News / Reddit — not article platforms, but links posted in relevant discussions get crawled almost immediately. Engage genuinely; don't spam.
  • Qiita / Note / Zenn — Japanese tech platforms with strong SEO. English tech content is accepted.
  • Your own blog — the hub everything points back to. Mine: MyToolster Blog.

2. Google indexing speed ranking

Not all platforms get indexed equally fast. From my experience:

⚡ Indexes very fast: Hacker News, Reddit, Dev.to, Substack
🐢 Indexes very slowly: Medium

So: put time-sensitive content on the fast four. Use Medium for the long game — its domain authority pays off once indexed, but don't expect it next week.

3. How to increase indexing probability

  1. Comment under homepage articles on the same platform. Engage with popular posts in your niche — that's how crawlers (and readers) discover your profile and your articles.
  2. Cross-link your articles across platforms. Each post should link to its siblings elsewhere. It builds a web, not isolated pages — and every link is a crawl path.
  3. Link to your platform profiles from your own website. Add your Medium/Substack/Hashnode/dev.to profile links to your site footer. It tells Google these accounts legitimately belong to you.
  4. Try different article formats. Tutorials, comparisons, deep dives, opinion pieces — varied formats get picked up by different discovery surfaces.
  5. Keep quality high. Thin content doesn't get indexed and doesn't rank. One solid tutorial beats five filler posts.

4. FAQ

Q: What if my account gets banned?

A: Keep backup accounts and warm them up gradually. Don't put all your content on a single account — spread across platforms and have spares ready. New accounts should start with genuine engagement before publishing promotional content.

Q: What if my article gets 0 reads?

A: Two things: increase the probability of getting indexed (see the five rules above), and keep publishing more high-quality articles. Zero-read articles are normal at first — indexing compounds, and volume plus quality is the only reliable fix.

The setup that works

One hub (this blog) + every platform linking to it + platforms linking to each other + your main site linking to all profiles. Publish on the fast-indexing platforms first, let Medium compound in the background. Write once, adapt per platform, cross-link everything.

That's the whole playbook. It costs $0.
