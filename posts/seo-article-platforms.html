SEO Backlinks: Article Platforms Where You Can Publish Yourself

SEO · Guide · By MyToolster

Directories are the slow lane for backlinks. Article platforms are the fast lane: you publish, you get the link, and good content gets indexed within days. Here are the platforms I've actually published on — with notes on what works and what to watch out for.

The lineup

  • Medium — high domain authority, fast indexing. Set your profile bio with your links, organize posts into public Lists, and engage under already-indexed articles in your niche.
  • Dev.to — developer-focused, great for tutorials. Canonical URLs supported for cross-posting.
  • Hashnode — dev blogging with your own subdomain. Warning: their AutoMod aggressively flags promotional "tool list" posts on free subdomains — write tutorials, keep same-domain links to 1–2 per post.
  • Substack — newsletter + blog in one. Posts get emailed to subscribers and stay publicly indexed.
  • Blogger — Google's own platform, indexes fast. Simple editor, zero friction.
  • Qiita / Note / Zenn — Japanese tech platforms with strong SEO. English tech content is accepted; worth it if you want Japanese search traffic.
  • This blog — your own domain is the hub everything points back to.

Five rules that actually matter

  • Engage, don't just publish. Comment on popular articles in your niche on each platform — that's how people (and crawlers) find you.
  • Cross-link your articles. Each post should link to its siblings on other platforms. It builds a web, not isolated pages.
  • Link back from your own site. Add your platform profiles to your website footer — it tells Google these accounts are legitimately yours.
  • Vary the format. Tutorials, comparisons, deep dives, opinion pieces — don't publish the same shape twice in a row.
  • Quality is the filter. Zero-read articles are normal at first. Keep publishing good content; indexing compounds.

The setup that works

One hub (this blog) + every platform linking to it + platforms linking to each other + your main site linking to all profiles. Write once, adapt per platform, cross-link everything.

That's the whole playbook. It costs $0.
